# Guided lesson storyboards

**Draft P00.** These are proposed lessons. No animations or verified numerical lesson states exist yet.

All lessons operate on the same case model as Explore. Store versioned initial states, parameter changes, captions, camera/section cues and expected outcomes. Include play/pause, scrub, next/back, restart, captions, reduced motion and Explore this moment. Preserve the user's previous sandbox and allow return to the lesson. Optional narration may follow an approved script; captions must stand alone.

## 1. Factors that govern where poles break

**Question:** Why does a pole not always fail at groundline?

1. Introduce a sound tapered pole, the applied tip force and changing bending demand down its length.
2. Contrast demand with the varying section resistance. Show the governing region from the calculation.
3. Change taper and add a local weak region. Move the section to see why the governing location changes.
4. Rotate the horizontal load around an asymmetric defect; compare the resulting governing load and location in A/B.
5. Compare timber failure with a qualified soil/foundation limit once implemented. Explain the ideal-restraint assumption if using the early benchmark.

**Try it:** move a defect and predict which region will govern, then reveal the result. This is optional exploration, not a mandatory answer form.

**Evidence:** verified tapered/asymmetric section analysis and supported failure criteria. Show first-limit indication initially. A snap/fall sequence is illustrative until fracture and post-failure dynamics are implemented and validated.

## 2. How decay influences pole strength

**Question:** Why is the visible size of a hollow region not enough to determine capacity?

1. Start with equal poles and establish a comparison baseline.
2. Introduce fibre deterioration without removing all material. Explain the chosen property changes and their evidence status.
3. Compare central loss with off-centre or outer-fibre loss; match the comparison quantity explicitly.
4. Extend a decayed region along the pole and move the section through it.
5. Rotate the load to show direction dependence and compare estimated limiting loads with their uncertainty.

**Try it:** retain the same missing area but move it. Explain which differences the current model resolves and which it cannot, including thin-wall or split failure where unsupported.

**Evidence:** independently checked remaining-section integration; approved deterioration-property relationships for quantitative claims. The progression is a sequence of assumed conditions, not elapsed years or a universal decay-growth law.

## 3. How the UB1000 device determines pole capacity

**Question:** How does an inspection contribute to a strength assessment?

1. Show the outside of a pole and mark a test plane.
2. Place probes using the approved procedure and animate an acoustic pulse across the pole.
3. Compare representative received signals and explain the observed features.
4. Move to another inspection height and show the stated coverage and uncertainty.
5. Walk through measurement → interpreted condition → approved remaining-strength relationship → structural assessment using pole geometry and loads.
6. Reveal the sandbox's known interior separately from the inferred condition. Show why they need not match perfectly.

**Try it:** change an internal condition and observe the simulated response, then compare interpretation with hidden truth.

**Evidence:** public sources support the conceptual sequence; device-specific traces, scales, probe placement, inference and capacity equations require owner-approved references. Mark synthetic signals and slowed acoustic time. Do not imply that UB1000 directly applies a breaking load or produces an exact 3D scan.

## 4. How the UB1000 Safe to Climb tool identifies poles that may be hazardous to climb

**Question:** What information is needed before an inspection workflow can support a climbing decision?

1. Show the existing pole condition and supported loads.
2. Introduce the climbing scenario and its prescribed actions once the actual protocol is supplied.
3. Show how the approved workflow uses measurement quality, condition and applicable capacity margins.
4. Compare a relevant reference case with an adverse or incomplete-information case.
5. Explain the permitted outcomes and escalation steps using the approved wording.

**Try it:** explore an adverse condition or missing measurement and see why more information or an alternative work method may be needed.

**Evidence:** actual Safe to Climb rules, limitations, reference decisions and technical review. Before these are supplied, use a conceptual storyboard with no numerical thresholds or operational clearance. A green simulated stress map alone must never act as permission to climb.

## Lesson acceptance

Each step must be reproducible from saved inputs, use the current supported model and retain its basis when paused or explored. Recheck lesson conclusions after solver/data revisions. Captions must match results, even when the governing location changes. Paused or failed calculations must not advance to an unsupported conclusion. Scrubbing must not leak damage/load history between cases.
